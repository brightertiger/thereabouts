# Market check: publish a narrow experiment; do not build the universal utility yet

**Verdict — judgement:** A small, explicitly approximate offline reverse-geocoding package is worth publishing as a preview for batch analytics and private/local applications. The proposed “any location hint → place” product is **not justified by the evidence**: the individual jobs are well served, the inputs have incompatible meanings, and demand for bringing all of them together offline remains unproven. Spend the next week finding users for the existing capability; do not make a much larger ODbL database and a collection of adapters the prerequisite for learning whether anyone will use it.

This is a recommendation about scope and investment, not a finding that the implementation is worthless or that a business has been validated.

**Evidence date and citation convention:** **Every external source linked in this report was accessed on 2026-10-01**; this date applies to every linked claim and every row of the tables. Publication/release dates are stated separately. “Observed” means present in the cited primary source or a direct registry/API response, not independently benchmarked. “Inference” and “Proposal” identify this report's judgement. “Unverified” means the investigation did not establish the claim. Download counts are observations of the reporting service, not user counts.

**Local baseline, read 2026-10-01:** [README](../README.md) reports a 53.06 MB installed table, 21.35 MB ZIP, 13.54 µs warm scalar median and 436,159 points/s for its batch workload. Its country/state/city mismatch rates are respectively 0.083/0.212/0.763% on area-uniform land and 0.235/0.606/5.212% near settlements. These compare the cell-centre answer with the **same sources evaluated at the original point**, not independent geographic truth. [DESIGN](DESIGN.md) describes the current string-based result and centre-label semantics. [IDEA_EVALUATION](IDEA_EVALUATION.md) already covers the original competitors and algorithms; this report does not repeat that benchmark. [DATA_PLAN](DATA_PLAN.md) estimates **250–800 MB installed, 90–350 MB compressed and 10–18 engineer-days** for the replacement data work. Those are estimates, not measurements of a finished new pack. No package benchmark or data build was run for this report.

## Four findings that drive the verdict

1. **There is a market for the pieces; that does not establish a market for the bundle.** Observed latest reported monthly downloads include **405,810** for `reverse-geocoder`, **825,741** for `geonamescache`, **1,220,227** for `pgeocode`, **5,105,627** for `timezonefinder` and **30,384,826** for `pycountry`. These are meaningful signs of established distribution, with large differences between specialists. They cannot be added into a market size: installations overlap, and automated builds and dependencies contribute. A working organisation-specific wrapper, Hack Club's geocoder, does combine address and IP lookups, but uses Google/IPinfo and a cache. **Inference:** integration convenience is plausible; a demand for one offline world database spanning all proposed inputs is a separate hypothesis. Sources: [reverse-geocoder downloads](https://pypistats.org/packages/reverse-geocoder), [geonamescache downloads](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/geonamescache/recent), [pgeocode downloads](https://pypistats.org/packages/pgeocode), [timezonefinder downloads](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/timezonefinder/recent), [pycountry downloads](https://pypistats.org/packages/pycountry), [download methodology](https://pypistats.org/faqs/), [Hack Club implementation](https://github.com/hackclub/geocoder).

2. **The broad promise combines different questions, not just different encodings.** MaxMind describes IP coordinates as approximate and supplies an accuracy radius; libphonenumber explicitly warns that people may have numbers belonging to countries where they do not live. Timezone identifiers describe timekeeping regions, and postal lookup already needs country context in `pgeocode`. **Inference:** a single unconditional `city/state/country` answer will hide uncertainty and sometimes invent precision. The valuable shared abstraction would be identified entities, candidate matches and relationships with provenance, not a universal conversion through one point. Sources: [MaxMind database semantics](https://dev.maxmind.com/geoip/docs/databases/city-and-country/), [libphonenumber FAQ](https://github.com/google/libphonenumber/blob/master/FAQ.md), [IANA theory](https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tzdb/theory.html), [pgeocode contract](https://pgeocode.readthedocs.io/en/latest/).

3. **The proposed data upgrade makes the adoption proposition harder as well as potentially improving coverage.** Overture explicitly licenses its divisions theme under ODbL. PyPI's defaults are **100 MB per uploaded file and 10 GB per project**. The local plan's 90–350 MB compressed estimate straddles that upload limit; installed size is not the limit. Meanwhile, `timezonefinder` has already separated its data distribution: the current `timezonefinder-data` wheel is **32,653,639 bytes**, version **3.2026.4**, uploaded **2026-09-21**. **Inference:** a 250–800 MB installation is acceptable for some servers, but weakens the casual `pip install` and small-device story. The maintenance/distribution work is part of the product, not an incidental build step. Sources: [Overture attribution](https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/), [PyPI limits](https://docs.pypi.org/project-management/storage-limits/), [data-package metadata](https://pypi.org/pypi/timezonefinder-data/json), [upstream separation issue](https://github.com/jannikmi/timezonefinder/issues/446), [local estimate](DATA_PLAN.md).

4. **The strongest niche is better offline place context, not unprecedented unification.** Who's On First already has place identities, hierarchy, names, concordances and downloadable SQLite distributions; Pelias/Placeholder and Go tooling make them searchable. Pelias has **3,591 GitHub stars**, Placeholder **343**, and commercial stewardship through Geocode Earth. In contrast, two relevant Overture experiments have **9 stars** (`brad-richardson/overture-geocoder`) and **23 stars** (`developmentseed/gazet`); the former explicitly says its unified Places/address v2 production API is paused. **Inference:** there is both serious incumbent infrastructure and a long tail of small experiments. Neither “nobody has thought of this” nor “adding all inputs will attract users” survives this comparison. Low stars and a paused interface do not prove commercial failure. Sources: [WOF downloads](https://whosonfirst.org/download/), [WOF properties](https://whosonfirst.org/docs/properties/wof/), [Pelias repository metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/pelias/pelias), [Placeholder metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/pelias/placeholder), [Pelias stewardship](https://pelias.github.io/pelias/), [Overture geocoder](https://github.com/brad-richardson/overture-geocoder), [its metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/brad-richardson/overture-geocoder), [gazet](https://github.com/developmentseed/gazet), [its metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/developmentseed/gazet).

It demonstrates interest in a self-hosted edge/on-prem maps appliance. The bundle includes maps and routing, not phone/IP/postal-to-place conversion; the submitter is the builder.
- [SFO Museum's Go geocoder](https://github.com/sfomuseum/geocoder) states a concrete need for language/tooling integration and custom filtering. Useful evidence for packaging and entity-model needs; not evidence for this exact Python product.
- [DuckDB's H3 extension adoption](https://duckdb.org/community_extensions/extensions/h3) and [published spatial-join example](https://duckdb.org/2025/08/08/spatial-joins) support analytics infrastructure demand. They do not measure willingness to install this particular precomputed table.
- A [first-person blog about localised Immich place names](https://inktrace.rxchi1d.me/en/posts/container-platform/immich-geodata-zh-tw/) appeared in search with a description of a language/data-customisation workflow. **Unverified beyond the indexed excerpt:** the full page returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch. It is a follow-up lead, not evidence counted in the verdict.

### Absent or unverified evidence

- No verified request was found for **all** of lat/lon, spatial encodings, names, ISO/admin codes, postal codes, IPs, phones, airports, ports and timezone names in one offline library. The two-input examples above should not be stretched into that conclusion.
- No buyer interview, installation trial, switching commitment, sponsor commitment, revenue, retention or willingness-to-pay evidence for **Thereabouts** was obtained. No external outreach was performed.
- No verified request from an air-gapped institution for this particular combination, and no verified AI-agent customer requesting a local Python all-hint resolver. Existing agent-oriented geospatial projects show supply and adjacent usage, not demand for this product.
- Some relevant Reddit results, including a direct offline package request and an Immich neighbouring-village complaint, were available only as search-index excerpts when full opens failed. They were not used as independently inspected core evidence.

**Inference:** the narrow need is credible. The broader idea currently has a supply-side rationale (“these things can be connected”) and limited integration evidence, rather than demonstrated demand for the whole bundle.

## The case against building it

**Judgement, stated strongly:** the broad project risks becoming an unpaid global data-maintenance operation whose headline features mostly wrap mature packages. Its differentiator would be reconciliation quality, but the proposed interface makes that quality unusually hard to define and prove. There is presently no evidence that enough users value that reconciliation to accept the heavier data, licence and trust costs.

The following are concrete reasons to stop expansion, rather than reasons to polish the pitch:

1. **The common result can be misleading.** A country/subdivision code identifies an area, not a city. A timezone can cover many cities. An airport record contains an associated city label, without promising a municipality-boundary test. Phone numbering metadata is not residence. IP coordinates carry uncertainty. These distinctions follow the [ISO wrapper's scope](https://github.com/pycountry/pycountry), [IANA model](https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tzdb/theory.html), [airport fields](https://github.com/mborsetti/airportsdata), [phone FAQ](https://github.com/google/libphonenumber/blob/master/FAQ.md) and [MaxMind schema](https://dev.maxmind.com/geoip/docs/databases/city-and-country/). **Inference:** forcing all of them through coordinates and then the H3 table discards useful identity and uncertainty; it can also replace a provider's named city with a different nearest settlement. Explicit typed resolvers and nullable/candidate results are necessary, reducing the apparent “one magic function” benefit.

2. **A shared catalogue cannot manufacture IP accuracy.** MaxMind's own estimates for its **GeoIP products** are approximately **99.8% country accuracy**, and for US IPs **80% state and 66% city within a 50 km radius**. These are vendor estimates, not a GeoLite2 global city guarantee and not comparable to this repo's quantization mismatch rates. [Primary accuracy statement](https://support.maxmind.com/knowledge-base/articles/maxmind-geolocation-accuracy). **Inference:** converting such an estimate into a resolution-8 cell makes it look precise without adding information. IP lookup belongs in a separately qualified adapter, if anywhere.

3. **The download/install burden is material, although not fatal.** The **250–800 MB installed** and **90–350 MB compressed** sizes remain [planning estimates](DATA_PLAN.md). Pure transfer time for the estimated archive at **10 Mbit/s** is **72–280 seconds**, calculated as bytes × 8 / bandwidth, before retries/extraction. This is a scenario, not a measured user's connection. Mmap does not mean all installed bytes become resident RAM; new-pack cold start and RSS are unverified. [PyPI's 100 MB file limit](https://docs.pypi.org/project-management/storage-limits/) concerns the compressed uploaded distribution. **Inference:** explicit data installation, checksums, regional selection or external archives are manageable for pipeline users, but every extra step weakens the convenience proposition. No compression/size-limit increase should be promised before measurement.

4. **ODbL removes an easy low-friction licensing story.** Overture [divisions are ODbL](https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/); OSM land [also carries ODbL](https://osmdata.openstreetmap.de/info/license.html). The licence permits commercial use and excludes the database-operating computer programs from its scope (§§3.1, 2.3). Public use of a derivative database triggers share-alike and applicable access obligations (§§4.4–4.6); private/internal use is treated differently. It does **not** automatically require every user's application or all business data to be open sourced. [ODbL text](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/). **Inference:** the combined shipped table should follow the plan's derivative-database treatment, but downstream enrichment/join scenarios can require careful classification. Some organisations will avoid that review. No rejection-rate estimate or legal opinion on a specific customer's combined database was obtained. A permissive software licence cannot erase the data obligations.

5. **The wider scope defeats a single simple update cadence.** GeoNames publishes [daily extracts](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/readme.txt); Overture documents **monthly releases** and public retention of only **60 days/two monthly releases**, so old source URLs are not a durable reproduction archive. [Release policy](https://docs.overturemaps.org/release-calendar/). The current [local plan](DATA_PLAN.md) proposes quarterly shipped packs. MaxMind lists GeoLite City updates **Tuesday and Friday**, and its [developer documentation](https://dev.maxmind.com/geoip/geolite2-free-geolocation-data/) requires deletion of old GeoLite databases within **30 days of a new release**. [Update schedule](https://support.maxmind.com/knowledge-base/articles/download-and-update-maxmind-databases). **Inference:** yearly frozen IP data is not a viable default under that source's stated terms. A quarterly geographic snapshot, independently updated IP pack, telephone metadata and timezone rules are multiple maintenance tracks. Adapters to user-supplied data are safer operationally than promising one universally bundled snapshot. Licence compatibility and redistribution rights for a proposed IP source remain to be established before bundling.

6. **The accuracy claim is not yet a reason to trust a new package.** The [README](../README.md) explicitly limits its measured error rates to source-oracle quantization, and [DATA_PLAN](DATA_PLAN.md) has unexecuted acceptance targets for new data. **Inference:** more complete inputs may improve results but can also introduce new naming, hierarchy, overlap and political-policy conflicts. Users see “wrong city,” not whether the H3 index faithfully reproduced a source rule. Independent held-out labels and a public error catalogue matter more than a small warm-lookup latency figure. The upgraded pack's accuracy was not verified in this task.

7. **Convenience already has a low price.** The specialist libraries, GeoNames/WOF identifiers, and free API allowances above often solve the user's actual one or two tasks. **Inference:** someone needing airport→country can already obtain city, subdivision and timezone from a sub-megabyte `airportsdata` wheel ([artifact metadata](https://pypi.org/pypi/airportsdata/json), wheel **931,164 bytes**). Requiring a much larger boundary pack for that job would make the product less convenient. Optional dependencies reduce this problem but leave a wrapper whose advantage must be demonstrated.

8. **A useful package is not automatically a sustainable business.** Download counts can remain high while releases stall, as the trajectories below show. **Inference:** the likely durable value is data QA, correction handling and reliable updates. Those create recurring work before they create recurring revenue. No evidence found supports a revenue forecast for this project.

## The narrowest version worth publishing

**Proposal:** position the first release as **“offline city/state/country context for batches of coordinates and H3 cells, with a dated dataset and explicit approximation.”** Publish an experimental release for users who accept that contract. Do not sell it as authoritative municipal membership, address geocoding or a person's current location.

- **Keep:** latitude/longitude lookup, bounded batch operation, explicit offline installation, pinned snapshots, per-pack source/licence manifest, transparent nulls and documented city-selection policy. The [existing README](../README.md) already describes much of this contract; the proposed release still needs normal release validation.
- **Add only if needed to test the distinctive use case:** direct resolution-8 H3 input and a documented way for analytics users to consume labels. Coarser cells need an explicit centre/candidate policy; they cannot silently imply uniform coverage. Defer a new storage/export implementation until a tester actually needs it.
- **Data:** offer the existing small pack as a clearly labelled preview with its known limitations. If the pipeline produces a validated detailed pack, make its actual size/licence visible before download and test whether users prefer it. Do not imply that the small pack is a quality substitute for fixed coverage defects, or that the larger pack has already passed the plan's gates. Avoid promising two permanent world datasets before users justify maintaining both.
- **Keep semantic distinctions:** distinguish a selected/nearest settlement from actual containing administration and expose provenance. If future entity joins are justified, return namespace-qualified source IDs rather than relying on city strings. This recommendation does not require building a universal identity graph before the preview.
- **Cut from v1:** IP and phone inputs; postal and airport/port catalogues; timezone-name→city; currency enrichment; fuzzy/global name disambiguation; multilingual search; full addresses; a special AI/MCP product. H3/geohash/full plus-code decoding is not a new data market; geohash/plus-code convenience wrappers can wait.
- **Possible next experiment:** country-scoped exact name/code→candidate-place lookup, but only if users show a real join they cannot conveniently accomplish with `geonamescache`/WOF. Preserve ambiguity. This is the smallest expansion for which the research found a direct forward+reverse request.

**Stop condition — judgement:** if testers only want nearest-city results and an existing library meets their needs, contribute fixes or publish the builder/benchmark as a reference project. There is no obligation to turn one day's work into a maintained product. If testers need accurate municipal boundaries, evaluate that specific improvement; adding IPs or currencies will not answer their objection.

An unmet threshold does not prove no market exists; it means the evidence does not justify the next investment yet. Do not count the owner's enthusiasm, the other pipeline agent's progress, PyPI's automated downloads, or this report as a user commitment.

## What could not be found or verified

- No exhaustive all-language competitor census, and no proof that an all-input offline equivalent does not exist. Searched combinations covered reverse/forward offline Python, postal/IP/phone unification, WOF/Pelias, Overture gazetteers, H3 public tables, DuckDB, and forum/issue demand. Search ranking creates selection bias.
- No unique-user counts, retention, download-to-user conversion, customer counts, revenues, maintainer income or hours. PyPI Stats' exact rolling-window cutoff was not independently established. Its direct live responses were preferred over older cached search snippets.
- No comparable independent accuracy, cold-start, RAM or throughput measurements across the competitors. Vendor/upstream performance and accuracy claims are labelled as such. Open issue counts and release age are not quality scores.
- No new-pack world size, compressed artifact size, independent city accuracy, refresh cost, or acceptance-gate result. The other agent's ongoing pipeline work was not changed or represented as complete.
- No authoritative universal mapping from a timezone/phone/IP to one city, nor a verified licence-compatible scheme to redistribute every proposed source together. Specific downstream ODbL database classifications and IP redistribution permissions were not legally reviewed.
- No verified established Python port-code leader. No current usable global H3→city/state/country public table was confirmed: the identified country-table route returned 404. Placeholder's documented data route returned 403 to HEAD; neither response proves the whole project/data product is unavailable.
- No verified air-gapped or AI-agent buyer for the broad idea, and no willingness-to-pay evidence. Several promising demand threads are old; two Reddit full-page fetches failed, so their search excerpts were excluded from the core argument.
